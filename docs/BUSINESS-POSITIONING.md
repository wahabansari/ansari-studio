# Business Positioning — Ansari Product Engineering

**Created:** 30 October 2026
**Scope:** How the website should position the business, who it is for and how the offer turns into a conversation.
**Constraint:** Every claim below is supported by work already described in this repository. No invented clients, testimonials, certifications, rankings or performance metrics.

## 1. What the business actually sells

Ansari Product Engineering is a small, senior product engineering practice. It does three things, supported by three adjacent services:

| Core pillar | What the client buys | Primary page |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Software & product modernization | An existing application that is faster, supportable and no longer blocking the business | `/services/modernization` |
| AI & workflow automation | Fewer manual handoffs between the tools a business already uses | `/services/automation-ai` |
| Custom software development | A new web product, internal tool or SaaS MVP built to a written scope | `/services/product-engineering` |
| Dashboards & client portals | One place for the numbers or self-service tasks that currently live in spreadsheets and email | `/services/dashboards-portals` |
| API & system integrations | Systems that exchange data reliably instead of through copy-paste | `/services/api-integrations` |
| Maintenance & ongoing development | A dependable monthly capacity for fixes, updates and new features | `/services/maintenance-retainer` |

The commercial centre of gravity is the first three. The last three exist because real projects create them: a modernization uncovers an integration, an automation needs a dashboard, a launch needs maintenance.

## 2. Who this is for

### Primary buyer profiles

1. **The operator of a growing business with ageing software.**
   - Runs a live product, portal or internal system that still works but has become slow, fragile or hard to change.
   - Has a small or stretched technical team, or no in-house team.
   - Cares about uptime and revenue more than framework fashion.

2. **The founder or product lead shipping a new software product.**
   - Has a validated problem and needs a credible first version without hiring a full team.
   - Wants a written scope, visible progress and code they own.

3. **The operations lead drowning in manual work.**
   - Leads, invoices, reports or onboarding move between email, spreadsheets and a CRM by hand.
   - Needs a reliable workflow, not an AI demo.

4. **The agency that needs senior delivery capacity.**
   - Has sold work it cannot staff.
   - Needs white-label engineering that protects the client relationship.

### Secondary profiles

- Healthcare and clinic operations that need booking, patient-facing sites or internal workflow improvements.
- E-commerce and retail teams connecting storefronts, payments and fulfilment.
- Professional-services firms replacing spreadsheet reporting with a controlled dashboard.

### Explicit non-fit

- Buyers shopping only on the lowest hourly rate.
- Projects that need a large team from day one.
- Requests to publish invented case studies, certifications or guarantees.
- Regulated work that requires certifications this practice does not hold.

Being clear about non-fit protects delivery quality and keeps the sales conversation honest.

## 3. Positioning statement

> For growing companies whose software or operations have become the bottleneck, Ansari Product Engineering modernizes the systems they already depend on, automates the manual work around them and builds the custom products they need next — with a written scope, visible progress and full ownership at handover.

### Short version (homepage / social)

> Upgrade your software. Automate your operations. Grow faster.

### Category

Product engineering and software modernization for growing businesses. Not a generalist web agency, not a staff-augmentation marketplace, not an AI tool vendor.

### Differentiators that are true today

1. **Senior people do the work.** No account layer between the buyer and the engineer.
2. **Written scope before work starts.** Deliverables, assumptions, exclusions and milestones are agreed in writing.
3. **Small, reversible releases.** Modernization happens while the product stays live; no big-bang rewrite by default.
4. **You own everything.** Code, repositories, documentation and service accounts transfer at handover.
5. **One point of contact.** Clear ownership however many specialists are involved.
6. **Business outcomes, not framework theatre.** The question is always what the work removes or unlocks.

## 4. Messaging hierarchy

### The problem stack

Most buyers arrive with one of three symptoms:

1. **The software is slowing the business down.** Slow pages, fragile releases, hard-to-hire technology.
2. **The team is buried in manual work.** Copy-paste between tools, leads waiting in inboxes, reports rebuilt by hand.
3. **The systems do not talk to each other.** Data is re-keyed, numbers disagree and nobody trusts the dashboard.

### The promise

> Less friction, fewer manual steps and software your team can keep using.

### The proof

- Real, named case studies with scope and outcome described qualitatively.
- A documented four-step process: discovery, written scope, build, handover.
- A named founder with a verifiable LinkedIn profile.
- A stated response time (one business day) the practice can keep.

### The offer ladder

| Step | Offer | Purpose | Page |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Discovery call (30 minutes) | Qualify the problem and decide whether a scoped engagement makes sense | `/contact#book` |
| 2 | Automation audit | Fixed-scope review of tools and workflows, ending in a prioritized shortlist; priced in writing | `/automation-audit` |
| 3 | Fixed-scope project | Defined outcome, written scope, agreed estimate | `/contact` |
| 4 | Monthly development | Ongoing capacity for live products and workflows | `/services/maintenance-retainer` |
| 5 | Agency partnership | White-label overflow delivery under the agency's brand | `/partners` |

There is no published price list. Pricing is quoted after scope is understood, and the site says so.

## 5. CTA system

One primary action, one secondary action, used consistently:

- **Primary: “Book a discovery call” → `/contact#book`**
  - The contact page opens the Cal.com calendar in place.
  - Used in the header, homepage hero, service heroes, article pages, insights index and footer.
- **Secondary: “Discuss your project” → `/contact`**
  - Sends the visitor to the contact form with service and engagement fields.
  - Used beside the primary action, not instead of it.

Contextual actions are allowed where they are genuinely more specific:

- “Request an audit” on `/automation-audit`.
- “Send a message” on the contact page (jumps to the form).
- “Discuss a similar project” on case studies.
- “Contact us” on legal pages.

Every other page-level CTA should resolve to one of the two standard actions.

## 6. Objections and responses

| Objection | Response on the site |
| --- | --- |
| “Will you force a rewrite?” | Modernization is framed as measured, incremental and reversible. A rewrite is one option, not the default. |
| “Will I be locked in?” | Code, accounts and documentation transfer at handover. No proprietary platform. |
| “How do I know you can do this?” | Real case studies, a described process and a named founder. No borrowed logos or invented metrics. |
| “How much will it cost?” | Scope is agreed first, then priced in writing. The site explains why a public price list would be misleading. |
| “How fast will you reply?” | Within one business day, stated consistently. |
| “Do you understand my industry?” | Industry examples are framed as experience, not as claims of specialist certification. |
| “Is AI going to replace my team?” | Automation is described as removing repetitive steps, with people kept in the loop for judgment calls. |

## 7. Sales narrative

A good first call follows the same arc as the site:

1. **What is slowing you down?** Get the symptom in the buyer's words.
2. **What have you already tried?** Avoid repeating a failed approach.
3. **What does success look like in business terms?** Time saved, errors removed, revenue unblocked, risk reduced.
4. **What is the smallest credible first step?** Audit, fixed-scope project or a defined monthly block.
5. **What happens next, and by when?** Written scope or a clear no.

### Qualification signals

- A named business problem with a cost or consequence.
- Access to a decision-maker.
- Willingness to share systems, access or data.
- A realistic timeline and budget range.
- A preference for ownership and maintainability.

### Disqualification signals

- “Just send a price” with no scope discussion.
- Requests for speculative free builds or unpaid pilots.
- A need for certifications or guarantees the practice cannot support.
- A decision process that requires invented proof.

## 8. Proof policy

The site may publish:

- Work the practice actually delivered, described in the client's approved language.
- Qualitative outcomes that the client has confirmed.
- Technology names that are visible in the delivered work or already documented in this repository.
- The founder's real name, role and LinkedIn URL.

The site must not publish:

- Invented clients, logos, testimonials or ratings.
- Numeric performance claims that were not measured and approved.
- Certifications, partnerships or awards that do not exist.
- Search rankings, traffic figures or revenue claims.
- A published price list or “free” offer that has not been approved.

Sunhub remains a draft case study and must not appear in navigation, the work grid, the sitemap or structured data until its screenshot and claims are approved.

## 9. Measurement

Positioning is working when the right conversations start, not when traffic rises alone. Track:

- Discovery-call bookings (`booking_click`).
- Contact form submissions (`contact_submit`).
- Audit requests (`audit_intent`, `audit_submit`).
- Case-study engagement (`case_study_open`).
- Which service pages precede an enquiry.
- Qualified-conversation rate, not just lead count.

Analytics is consent-gated; absence of data is preferable to non-consensual tracking.
